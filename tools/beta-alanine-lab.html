What this tool actually does

  • It runs one published model. Carnosine rises in proportion to the daily dose and falls in proportion to how far it is above baseline (Spelnikov and Harris 2019). The model is the authors' proposal and they say it needs validation.
  • Each study is fitted on its own. Six loading studies and four washout studies give different constants. The band shows how far they disagree; it is not a pooled estimate and not a confidence interval for you.
  • Grams of carnosine are a rough estimate. Studies measured one or two leg muscles. The tool spreads that figure over an estimated skeletal muscle mass, and carnosine differs between muscles and fiber types.
  • Costs are a snapshot. Prices were checked by hand in November 2026 and vary widely between vendors and from day to day.

Not checked, at all

  • You. Baseline carnosine and the response to the same dose vary widely from person to person, and the studies were mostly of young, healthy, active men.
  • Your product. Supplement labels are not verified here. Products differ in purity and in what else they contain.
  • Performance. Nothing here predicts an effect on exercise.
  • Safety. The position stand cited calls tingling the only reported side effect at recommended doses in healthy people. The tool repeats that sentence and a 2019 meta-analysis that found the same, notes that muscle taurine did not change in people, and makes no other safety claim.

A tingling band of "below the smallest dose tested" means untested, not shown to be free of tingling.

How to use
  1. Pick a protocol. Start from a study preset, or set up to four phases of daily dose, servings, form and length, then how long to follow the washout.
  2. Read the chart. The line is the middle of the band; the band runs from the 10th to the 90th percentile of the draws. Hover, tap or focus the chart to see which papers make each edge on that day.
  3. Check the shaded part. Where no study at a comparable dose ran that long, the band is shaded and the hover names the study it rests on.
  4. Add person-to-person variation to see the spread between individuals as well as between papers. It is off by default because it stretches one study's 4-week spread across the whole timeline.

Beta-alanine and creatine are separate supplements; the Creatine Lab covers creatine. Neither tool models taking the two together.
